Blood Basics

Understanding Blood Groups

Every person's blood belongs to a specific type based on markers on the surface of red blood cells. Together, the ABO system and the Rh factor define the 8 major blood groups used for safe transfusions.

ABO System

The ABO system groups blood into four main types: A, B, AB, and O. Type A has A markers, Type B has B markers, Type AB has both, and Type O has neither—determining which blood types can safely mix.

Rh Factor

The Rh factor is a protein on red blood cells. If you have it, your blood is Rh-positive (+); if you do not, it is Rh-negative (-). Rh-negative blood can be safely given to both positive and negative recipients of compatible ABO types.

Good to Know

Quick Facts

Essential takeaways about blood group compatibility and emergency preparedness.

O- in Emergencies

O-negative red blood cells lack A, B, and Rh markers, so O- can be given to anyone in urgent emergencies.

AB+ Universal Recipient

People with AB-positive blood can safely receive red blood cells from any of the eight blood groups.

Safe Transfusion Matching

Matching blood type matters for safe transfusion so the recipient's immune system accepts the donated blood.

Preparedness Matters

Knowing your blood group helps doctors act faster and lets you step forward quickly when matching blood is needed.
